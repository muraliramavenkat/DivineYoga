About Yoga.

A living family of traditions from India, bringing together attention, ethical living, breath, movement and contemplation.

Origins & background

Yoga has ancient roots in Indian philosophy and spiritual practice. It developed through many communities and teachings over centuries; there is no single founder or universally agreed starting date. Modern classes often emphasise postures, breathing and meditation, but yoga is broader than physical exercise. [1]

Traditions transmit knowledge through teachers and students, texts and lived practice. UNESCO inscribed Yoga on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016, recognising its Indian cultural heritage. This is cultural recognition, not a medical endorsement. [68]

Different ways to practise

Yoga may include āsana (posture), prāṇāyāma (breath regulation), meditation and chanting. Schools differ in emphasis, language and method. [68]

DivineYoga offers contemporary introductions to Hatha, Yin, Vinyasa, Power and Ashtanga, alongside body-area browsing and breathing guides. These are starting points for learning, not a complete representation of every lineage.

High-level benefits — with perspective

Research suggests yoga may support stress management, sleep, mental and emotional wellbeing, and balance. Results depend on the programme and person, and studies vary in quality. Research on a complete yoga programme does not prove that one posture or breath has the same benefit. [1]

Comfortable practice, suitable modifications and qualified guidance matter. Yoga is not a substitute for medical care. A body-area category does not make a posture suitable for pain or injury in that area. [1]

An optional opening

Saha Navavatu — a peace invocation

This traditional invocation asks for protection, nourishment, shared effort and understanding between teacher and student. It can open a learning or yoga session; it is not a required or universal yoga chant. Different traditions use different invocations. [67]

ॐ सह नाववतु ।
सह नौ भुनक्तु ।
सह वीर्यं करवावहै ।
तेजस्वि नावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै ।
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥

Transliteration

Oṃ saha nāv avatu
Saha nau bhunaktu
Saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai
Tejasvi nāv adhītam astu mā vidviṣāvahai
Oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ

English meaning

Om. May we both be protected.
May we both be nourished.
May we work together with energy.
May our learning be illuminating; may we have no hostility toward one another.
Om. Peace, peace, peace.

An original plain-English rendering of the traditional Sanskrit; translations vary. Text and teacher–student context: [67].

How to begin

Sit comfortably, settle into a natural breath, and read or chant softly if this suits your beliefs. Do not stretch one exhale to fit a whole line. Silent reflection is equally welcome. You might set the intention: “May I practise with patience and care.”
