History & overview

Reverence for the sun has longstanding roots in Indian religious life. The familiar exercise sequences also have a modern history: a historical review describes their popularisation by the Raja of Aundh in the 1920s and later yoga teachers. Ancient sun worship does not establish that every modern sequence is unchanged from antiquity. [75]

Sivananda, Ashtanga and other schools teach different versions. This page follows the broad twelve-position pattern with explicit beginner changes. [74]

Our version & how to start

Tabletop replaces Plank; controlled lowering replaces eight-point prostration; Cobra stays low; deep standing backbends and breath retention are omitted. This is not the exact Sivananda sequence or Ashtanga Sun A/B.

Learn one pass slowly, rest, then repeat with the opposite leg leading if comfortable. Two passes cover both sides. Extra breaths are welcome; synchronising every movement is optional. No dawn practice or sun-gazing is required.

Benefits & research facts

The movements combine weight-bearing, joint movement and coordination. Effort depends on pace, range and modifications; an adapted sequence may be much lighter than a study protocol.

An eight-week randomised study involving 44 overweight or obese female university students reported improvements in several fitness and stress measures in the training group. The narrow sample and inactive comparison limit generalisation, including to older adults or people with health conditions. It does not validate this website’s modified sequence. [76]

A systematic review of 14 studies reported promising physical and psychological findings, but included varied interventions and called for larger trials with standardised methods. Avoid guaranteed weight-loss, detox or disease-cure claims. [77]

The 12-stage sequence

One pass · then change lead leg

01

Mountain / prayer

Tāḍāsana · optional Añjali Mudrā

Stand evenly; hands may meet at the chest.

Breathing: Natural breath.

Adapt & transition: Keep feet comfortably apart.

02

Gentle upward reach

Ūrdhva Hastāsana variation

Lengthen upward; lift arms only within comfort.

Breathing: Inhale if comfortable.

Adapt & transition: Keep ribs easy; no backward arch is needed. Image shows the standing base, not the optional arm lift.

03

Soft-knee forward fold

Uttānāsana variation

Hinge at the hips and bend the knees.

Breathing: Exhale.

Adapt & transition: Rest hands on blocks; do not pull the head toward the shins.

04

Left foot back · low lunge

Aśva Sañcalanāsana variation

Step the left foot back; lower that knee. Right foot stays forward.

Breathing: Inhale.

Adapt & transition: Pad the knee and place hands on blocks. If stepping is difficult, lower to hands and knees and place the foot from there.

05

Hands and knees

Tabletop · preparatory position

Bring the right knee back beside the left.

Breathing: Breathe freely.

Adapt & transition: Hands beneath shoulders; knees beneath hips. This replaces the source’s plank and breath hold.

Full posture guide & precautions ↗
06

Supported transition to the floor

Modified lowering · no fixed Sanskrit name

From knees, bend elbows and lower slowly to the belly only if controlled.

Breathing: Exhale without strain.

Adapt & transition: Image shows the start. If lowering is difficult, omit the floor backbend and go from Tabletop to Child’s Pose instead.

Full posture guide & precautions ↗
07

Low Cobra

Bhujaṅgāsana

Lift the chest a little, then lower with control.

Breathing: Inhale to lift.

Adapt & transition: Keep elbows soft and neck long; use back muscles rather than forcing upward with the hands.

08

Downward-facing Dog

Adho Mukha Śvānāsana

Return through hands and knees, tuck toes and lift hips.

Breathing: Exhale to lift hips.

Adapt & transition: Bend knees and leave heels raised. Use Tabletop or Child’s Pose if wrist loading or head-down posture is unsuitable.

09

Right foot forward · low lunge

Aśva Sañcalanāsana variation

Lower knees first if needed, then place right foot forward.

Breathing: Inhale.

Adapt & transition: Left knee can stay padded on the mat. Use blocks and a short stance.

Full posture guide & precautions ↗
10

Forward fold

Uttānāsana variation

Step left foot forward, keeping knees soft.

Breathing: Exhale.

Adapt & transition: Hands may remain supported; take extra breaths to reposition safely.

Full posture guide & precautions ↗
11

Rise and lengthen

Standing reach variation

With bent knees and a long back, return to standing.

Breathing: Inhale or breathe naturally.

Adapt & transition: Keep hands on hips for support, then optionally reach upward. Image shows the standing base.

12

Return to Mountain

Tāḍāsana

Lower arms and settle before changing sides.

Breathing: Exhale.

Adapt & transition: For the second pass, take the right foot back at step 4 and bring the left forward at step 9.

Do’s, don’ts & cautions

Do

  • Warm up gently and use a nonslip surface.
  • Learn shapes separately before linking them.
  • Use blocks, a padded knee or stable support.
  • Keep breathing; take extra breaths during transitions.
  • Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, numbness or unusual breathlessness.

Don’t

  • Do not chase speed, depth or a fixed round count.
  • Do not force backbends, straight knees or heels to the floor.
  • Do not twist a loaded knee when turning.
  • Do not push through fatigue or hold your breath.
  • Do not use a sequence as treatment for an injury.

Seek individual guidance for pregnancy, recent surgery, osteoporosis, glaucoma, cardiovascular concerns, dizziness or painful joints. Wrist/shoulder loading and head-down positions may need replacement. Severe chest pain, fainting or persistent breathing difficulty require medical attention. These are general editorial precautions; consult each posture’s guide and your clinician where relevant. [1]

Make it easier

Use a standing-only alternative: Mountain, a small upward reach, a supported hip hinge at a stable counter, then return to standing. Repeat only while comfortable. This is a simplified movement break, not a full Sun Salutation. For a formal chair version, see the Sivananda chair guide linked in the sources below.
