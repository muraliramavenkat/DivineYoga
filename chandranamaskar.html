History & overview

There is more than one Moon Salutation. Kripalu traces its own sequence to a collaboration in the late 1980s involving Megha Nancy Buttenheim, Deva Parnell, Patricia (Niti) Seip Martin and Bhumi Harriet Russell. This history belongs to that lineage, not necessarily every sequence using the name. [78]

“Cooling” and “lunar” describe traditional intention and style here, not proven temperature or hormone effects. You can practise at a comfortable time without waiting for a full moon.

Our standing adaptation

Inspired by Kripalu’s gentle standing version, this guide uses shallow Goddess, supported Triangle and a high lunge instead of Warrior I. It omits runner’s forward stretches and deep lateral squats. The source also offers a seated version. [79]

One cycle below covers both sides. Rest after it and decide whether to continue. “Gentle” describes the intention; it does not mean every posture suits every person.

Benefits & research facts

The flow offers comfortable hip and shoulder movement, lower-body effort and coordination. Slow pacing may feel settling; that experience varies. These are practical movement aims, not proven Moon-Salutation-specific clinical outcomes.

The research located for this guide includes a published protocol proposing a trial of immediate cardiovascular autonomic effects. A protocol describes a planned study; it is not evidence that an effect has occurred. Robust clinical conclusions cannot be drawn from it. [80]

General yoga research cannot prove that this particular flow treats insomnia, anxiety or hormonal problems. The teaching sources provide sequence and historical context, not clinical validation. [1] [78] [79]

Do’s, don’ts & cautions

Do

  • Warm up gently and use a nonslip surface.
  • Learn shapes separately before linking them.
  • Use blocks, a padded knee or stable support.
  • Keep breathing; take extra breaths during transitions.
  • Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, numbness or unusual breathlessness.

Don’t

  • Do not chase speed, depth or a fixed round count.
  • Do not force backbends, straight knees or heels to the floor.
  • Do not twist a loaded knee when turning.
  • Do not push through fatigue or hold your breath.
  • Do not use a sequence as treatment for an injury.

Seek individual guidance for pregnancy, recent surgery, osteoporosis, glaucoma, cardiovascular concerns, dizziness or painful joints. Wrist/shoulder loading and head-down positions may need replacement. Severe chest pain, fainting or persistent breathing difficulty require medical attention. These are general editorial precautions; consult each posture’s guide and your clinician where relevant. [1]

Make it easier

For balance concerns, use a stable chair and omit lunges and wide squats until assessed. A short seated alternative is comfortable sitting, an easy arm opening, a small side bend each way and a pause. It is an editorial alternative rather than the complete Moon sequence. See the source’s seated version for a teacher-led progression. [79]
